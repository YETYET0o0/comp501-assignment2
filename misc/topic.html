Retail Facial Recognition and CCTV under Privacy Act, in New Zealand

"Yes, you are being watched, even if no one is looking for you" (Krapp, 2022).

Rachel G, (2025, May 23). Asda’s facial recognition trial: a battle worth pursuing?[Photograph]

Ozie T, (2023, July 17). Should Retail Stores Buy Security Cameras?[Photograph]

In today’s society, Facial Recognition Technology (FRT) and Closed-Circuit Television (CCTV), are present almost everywhere people go: workplaces, train stations, shopping malls, restaurants, even universities where most people spend their days. In particular, the use of facial recognition technology and CCTV in retail environments, such as grocery stores and big-box stores, is increasing rapidly. They consist of a system of cameras installed at strategic points, transmitting real-time images to monitoring centres.

How does that help in retail sector?

These technologies help deter crime such as theft and improve operational safety. According to Foodstuffs North Island, “A facial recognition technology trial at 25 supermarkets for six months has reduced crime and prevented more than 100 serious physical and verbal assaults” (Rogers, 2024, para. 1). It also mentioned that FRT could be effective in deterring and responding to serious retail crime, suggesting that the technology may have a role to play in enhancing safety for staff and customers (MinterEllisonRuddWatts, 2025).

If so, what is the problem here?

However, at the same time, they raise significant privacy concerns regarding how shopper data is captured, stored, and used. Dr Jason Pallant, a marketing expert at RMIT University said, "We all agree that security and safety for retail staff is important, but that means every person who enters a supermarket is accepting their face being scanned" (McLeod, 2026, para. 4). On top of that, there is an example where FRT or CCTV caused privacy harm. In December 2025, New Zealand’s Office of the Privacy Commissioner reported that two PAK’nSAVE stores breached Information Privacy Principle 5 (IPP 5) of the Privacy Act 2020 (Office of the Privacy Commissioner, 2025). Security guards captured or shared CCTV footage and images of customers suspected of shoplifting without proper authorisation. It was published on social media, exposing the individuals to harassment and reputational damage. This case shows why the responsible use of retail FRT and CCTV is important under New Zealand’s Privacy Act 2020.

Our website

With these likely concerns raised by FRT and CCTV, this website is designed to investigate the concerns that FRT and CCTV are raising in modern society according to New Zealand’s Privacy Act 2020, and how these concerns can be addressed through appropriate legal, strategic, and operational measures. This investigation is essential because, although these surveillance technologies can help retailers prevent theft and improve safety, they also create significant risks relating to personal privacy, data security, and the unauthorised disclosure of sensitive information. In addition, this research is important for understanding how modern technology, including FRT and CCTV, should be used while ensuring legal compliance.
