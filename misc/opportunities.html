Technology Opportunities

Opportunities 1 Improving Staff and Customer Safety

Q. How can Facial Recognition Technology (FRT) improve staff and customer safety?

One of the most promising applications of Facial Recognition Technology (FRT) is enhancing the safety of both staff and customers at retail stores. When linked with Closed-Circuit Television (CCTV), FRT has the capability of identifying an individual who is on a security watch list. As soon as an image is matched against an individual's identity on a security watch list, it sends an alert to the retailer's staff. This potentially allows retailers to take action sooner in response to potential threats thereby reducing their exposure to future risks of harm.

Q. How can surveillance technology help prevent crime?

According to Wheatley (2024) CCTV and many other forms of surveillance are capable of assisting in preventing crime by deterring would-be criminals from engaging in criminal activity and providing law enforcement officials with evidence related to ongoing or past crimes. The author states that “the mere presence of surveillance cameras can act as a significant deterrent to criminal behaviour” (Wheatley, 2024, p. 2) .therefore suggesting that surveillance technologies can assist retailers in preventing undesirable events from occurring as opposed to simply documenting such occurrences once they have occurred.

Q. Is there evicence that FRT can improve safety in New Zealand supermarkets?

An example of how FRT has assisted retailers is through Foodstuffs North Island's trial implementation of FRT within 25 supermarket locations throughout New Zealand from February 2024 until September 2024. According to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner (2025), the trial was effective in reducing harmful behaviour, particularly serious violent incidents. Therefore, FRT presents a method for New Zealand retailers to enhance the level of safety associated with their supermarkets; especially considering the types of repeat offenses that involve individuals being placed upon a security watch list.

Q. Does the success of one FRT trial mean that every retailer should implement the technology?

We don't think so. The outcomes of one trial do not necessarily mean that FRT will provide equivalent levels of success across all retail establishments. Additionally, prior to implementing FRT, each retailer must assess if utilizing FRT is truly required, and whether less invasive alternatives can be used to achieve comparable levels of security.

Opportunities 2 Faster Identification and Response

The second opportunity created by using Facial Recognition Technology (FRT) is that it can help security teams quickly identify individuals of interest and respond to potential security incidents. CCTV Systems have traditionally been used to record video for viewing by security staff.

However, with FRT an individual’s face can be compared against a security watch list and alerts will be sent to Security Personnel once there has been a possible match. This may reduce the need for security staff to manually monitor CCTV footage for individuals on a watchlist, allowing them to focus more attention on potential security incidents. Retail NZ (2025) published a joint statement in which major New Zealand retailers expressed support for the fair and accurate use of FRT to help protect employees and customers from repeat offenders.

Q. What evidence demonstrates that FRT can identify individuals on security watch lists?

The statement also stated that FRT could play an important role in a comprehensive security program consisting of CCTV, security guards, employee training etc. Additionally, the Office of the Privacy Commissioner (2025) reported that during Foodstuffs North Island's FRT Trial 1,742 alerts were generated, of which 1,208 were confirmed matches. These results demonstrate that FRT was able to generate confirmed watchlist matches during a real-world retail trial. However, the figures alone do not prove that security staff responded more quickly.

Opportunities 3 Improving Crowd and Queue Management

Q. How can AI-powered CCTV improve crowd and queue management?

In addition to facial recognition, AI-powered CCTV systems can also be used to analyse crowd movement and queues. Video analytics using intelligent methods to analyse CCTV footage will allow organisations to measure the number of people within an area and determine which areas are most crowded as well as track line ups. Organisations could then use this data to create better staffing schedules, plan layouts and direct flow through their facilities.

Q. How can video analytics improve retail operations and customer experiences?

According to Connell et al. (2013), retail video analytics allows retailers to gain insight into customer traffic patterns and lines. They report that camera based analysis can assist in creating positive customer experiences, enhance store operations, and reduce operating expenses. For example, supermarkets could use checkout queue data to decide whether additional checkout lanes are needed during busy periods.

Q. How could AI-powered CCTV be used to manage crowds at public events?

Similar technologies could potentially be used at concerts, music festivals and sporting events to monitor crowd density and queue lengths. Event organizers could potentially recognize congested entrance points and modify staffing or entrance arrangements prior to having to deal with a possible over-crowded situation. In turn, this could lead to better attendee experiences and aid in more efficient crowd control; however, since Connell et al. (2013) focused primarily on retail environments, there is a need for more research regarding the uses of these technologies at large public events.
