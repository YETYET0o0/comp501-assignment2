Technology Opportunities

Opportunity 1: Improving Staff and Customer Safety

One of the most promising applications of Facial Recognition Technology (FRT) is enhancing the safety of both staff and customers at retail stores. When linked with Closed-Circuit Television (CCTV), FRT has the capability of identifying an individual who is on a security watch list. As soon as an image is matched against an individual's identity on a security watch list, it sends an alert to the retailer's staff. This potentially allows retailers to take action sooner in response to potential threats thereby reducing their exposure to future risks of harm.

According to Wheatley (2024) CCTV and many other forms of surveillance are capable of assisting in preventing crime by deterring would-be criminals from engaging in criminal activity and providing law enforcement officials with evidence related to ongoing or past crimes. The author states that “the mere presence of surveillance cameras can act as a significant deterrent to criminal behavior” (Wheatley, 2024, p. 2) .therefore suggesting that surveillance technologies can assist retailers in preventing undesirable events from occurring as opposed to simply documenting such occurrences once they have occurred.

An example of how FRT has assisted retailers is through Foodstuffs North Island's trial implementation of FRT within 25 supermarket locations throughout New Zealand from February 2024 until September 2024. In accordance with the Office of the Privacy Commissioner (2025) this trial resulted in empirical evidence demonstrating that the utilization of FRT was successful in decreasing instances of undesirable behaviors including those that were considered to be serious and violent. Therefore, FRT presents a method for New Zealand retailers to enhance the level of safety associated with their supermarkets; especially considering the types of repeat offenses that involve individuals being placed upon a security watch list.

The outcomes of one trial does not necessarily imply that FRT will provide equivalent levels of success across all retail establishments. Additionally, prior to implementing FRT, each retailer must assess if utilizing FRT is truly required, and whether less invasive alternatives can be used to achieve comparable levels of security.
