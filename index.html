Retail Facial Recognition And CCTV Under The Privact Act In New Zealand

Abstract

The integration of Artificial Intelligence (AI) into education is becoming increasingly common, with applications ranging from personalized learning systems to automated grading tools. This project explores various ways AI is being used in educational environments, highlighting its potential to improve teaching efficiency, support students with special needs, and tailor learning content to individual learners. However, alongside these benefits come important risks and challenges, such as data privacy concerns, algorithmic bias, and overdependence on technology. By examining the social, ethical, and technical implications of AI in education, this project aims to develop a comprehensive framework for responsible and sustainable AI integration in teaching and learning practices.

Summary

Topic

Facial Recognition Technology (FRT) and Closed-Circuit Television (CCTV) are increasingly used in retail to deter crime and improve safety, with reported reductions in assaults. However, at the same time, they raise significant privacy concerns about data capture, storage, and use. For example, New Zealand’s Office of the Privacy Commissioner reported that two PAK’nSAVE stores breached Privacy Principle of the Privacy Act 2020 (Office of the Privacy Commissioner, 2025), as security guards captured or shared CCTV footage and published on social media, exposing the individuals to harassment and reputational damage. Therefore, this website has been made to analyse whether FRT and CCTV are being used under New Zealand Privacy Act 2020.

Opportunities

Develoment of the time CCTV and Facial Recognition Technology (FRT) have a lot opportunities to impove for retailers and public spaces. especially for improving staff and customer safety, faster identification with response and improving crowd and queue management.

Risks

Although CCTV and Facial Recognition Technology (FRT) can improve security, they also create risks for invasion of privacy, misidentification and algorithmic bias, access and Misuse of CCTV footage.

Choices

This section examines the advantages and disadvantages of facial recognition system and CCTV to determine how they should be used. Also, how these technologies should be operated and restricted by regulation are considered to compare with current privacy code in New Zealand.
